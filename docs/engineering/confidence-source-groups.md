# Confidence groups and observed copies

Confidence counts publisher-domain groups for the delivered evidence on each
research target. Observed publisher IDs, shared DOI works and identical normalized
source bodies connect those groups transitively. The representative is stable
under source reordering. Body identity uses the existing NFKC/whitespace-normalized
digest; it adds no fuzzy similarity, fetch, model call or source attribution.

Every cited marker with an observed nonempty body keeps its group mapping,
including a later copy. A stronger delivered statement attached to that marker
must not disappear merely because a weaker statement from the same body was seen
first. Copies still contribute one group. Their observed publisher/work links
also apply to other articles in the same connected group, so a copy cannot bridge
two publishers and then let another article manufacture diversity. Empty bodies
do not create copy links or count as corroborating evidence.

The classification retains existing support thresholds, reviewed sentence/quote
pairing, per-target coverage, final sufficiency, conflict and synthetic-data gates.
This projection does not change the evidence ledger, reward eligibility, citations,
payments or retained reports. Domain/content grouping remains a conservative proxy;
it does not prove common ownership, independent corroboration or factual truth.

Regressions exercise actual ledger and sentence selection with controlled supplier
scores, all six orders of a weak/strong duplicate and another publisher, and all
120 orders of a transitive body/domain/DOI/publisher-ID graph. These are structural
offline checks, not an independent research-quality or live-model acceptance trial.
