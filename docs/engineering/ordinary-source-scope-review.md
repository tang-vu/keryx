# Ordinary summary review preserves source scope

A bounded comparison over retained excerpts exposed a concrete failure: a model
converted a publisher's paper-submission moderation policy into advice for a
customer's public API, and its separate numeric review still accepted the prose.
This is a semantic counterexample, not a representative production failure rate
or a claim that one model is generally better. Original reports stay immutable.

New ordinary evidence-only summaries require explicit review of the question's
subject and the statement's source scope. The reviewer supplies a bounded
supported-fact clause, `questionScope` and, for prose, `statementScope`. Related,
unrelated, unknown, missing or malformed question scope withholds support.
Generalized or unknown statement scope withholds the sentence even when a quote
supports a directly relevant fact. Numeric scores cannot override these checks,
and the existing reviewer still only reduces proposed support. Duplicate reviews
remain ambiguous and cannot grant delivery authority.

These are additional model judgments, not independent semantic verification.
Literal quote/version validation, source context, language, reward eligibility
and payment evidence retain their existing gates. The same 32KB review-input
limit includes the additional guidance and article title; entire rows are omitted
when they do not fit, with no source-context clipping or raised provider limits.

Ordinary selector enrollment determines this contract. Public JSON cannot turn it
off. Direct private, canary, finite-original, teaching and decision-brief contracts
keep their existing prompts. Ordinary web/API/MCP/CLI/bot engine users inherit the
summary behavior through their existing roles; no new request, funding, signing,
payment, storage or scheduler authority is added. Desktop/extension handoffs remain
within their existing roles. Current useful-answer and independent customer
acceptance rates require separate actual evidence.
