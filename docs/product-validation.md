# Keryx product validation

Authorized October 1, 2026: investigate specialized research and information-selling
infrastructure through narrow usable experiments. This is product work beyond bounty
eligibility. Neither direction has demonstrated independent repeat demand. Event speed
does not waive the existing security, payment, or mainnet release gates.

## Decision to make

Research sells a useful completed outcome. Infrastructure sells the ability for an
independent information provider to sell and deliver its own information to agents.
Keep one payment authority. Use a narrow research application as a reference client
where useful; do not build two broad products or a marketplace before validating demand.
Existing Operator/desktop work remains staged; this experiment adds no new expansion.

The owner selected the first research niche on October 1: choosing YouTube video
topics and developing useful production briefs for an English-language channel,
tentatively for children, with no reference channel supplied. Age remains unselected;
the owner requested a comparison before choosing. Format and production capabilities
remain open. This is an unvalidated hypothesis, not a demonstrated market or a promise
of virality. Use the [YouTube pilot](./youtube-research-pilot.md).
Inspect the current corpus before promising coverage. Do not invent paid
expertise, use demo/seed articles as independent publisher adoption, or sell access to
third-party public content without appropriate rights and terms.

## Experiment A: a useful research outcome

Recruit through owner-authorized contact. Ask about the participant's most recent
research task, existing method, time spent, and what decision the result supports.
Choose a real task with permission to send a sanitized question. Do not supply a
Keryx-themed question merely to make the demo work.

1. Record privately the task, intended deliverable and existing baseline method.
2. Inspect actual listed sources and free previews. Metadata suggests relevance;
   it does not establish answerability, accuracy, ownership or payable eligibility.
3. If the visible sources cannot support the task, record a corpus mismatch. Do not
   buy a package merely to create activity. The participant may choose the separately
   identified sponsored trial, whose result may still be unsupported.
4. When appropriate, use the existing sponsored web/MCP path. A caller-funded purchase
   requires the participant's deliberate authorization and existing buyer safeguards.
5. Have the participant inspect the answer and citations, then create or improve their
   own decision note/checklist. Separate human corrections from agent output.
6. Ask whether it was used, what needed correction, and whether they have a next task.

Proposed first sample: three genuine tasks in one segment. Record baseline and Keryx
time including review, factual/source errors, accepted output, assistance minutes,
participant-selected next task and payment willingness. These are proposed evidence
fields, not achieved counts or a statistically representative benchmark. Do not infer
retention from a prompted second demo or revenue from willingness to pay.

Continue the segment if independent participants use the output and bring further
work. Change the corpus or segment if the recurring failure is missing useful evidence.
Investigate delivery/UX if good evidence exists but participants cannot use the result.
An assisted service may be valid, but account for human work before claiming automation.

## Experiment B: an independent information seller

Find a provider with information it controls and an identifiable prospective buyer.
Current Keryx supports source/feed and research journeys; general arbitrary-API seller
onboarding is not demonstrated by this experiment. Never promise that capability.

1. Confirm the provider's actual asset, intended buyers, existing sale/delivery method,
   content rights and recurring integration/operations problem.
2. Use the current registration and ownership/payout flow with the provider's own
   compatible feed. Review its price, public preview and rights before publishing.
3. Inspect the actual resulting listing and preview. Distinguish discovery from a
   successfully registered payout authority and an eligible paid item.
4. An independent buyer requests a relevant task. Use a sponsored trial only with
   sponsorship clearly labeled; do not call it independent paid demand.
5. Inspect access, delivery, actual citations, rewards and pending/settled states.
   A registered source is not guaranteed to be bought or cited.
6. Ask whether the provider will maintain the integration and what operational work
   it removes. A fork, star or one assisted installation is not production adoption.

Proposed initial gate: one independently controlled compatible source and one genuine
buyer workflow, with integration effort and continued use documented. If providers
want buyers rather than payment tooling, that is a distribution problem, not proof
that adding SDK features will make the product useful.

## Evidence and privacy

Keep task questions, private repo context, job/recovery identifiers, full receipts,
participant identities and actual economics outside tracked public files. Obtain
participant consent before recording or publishing their identity/work. No external
contact, X posting or form submission is authorized by this document.

Capture separately: independent versus owner use; sponsored versus caller-funded;
testnet versus mainnet; accepted outcome versus completed request; and seller-reported
settlement versus independent confirmation. Receipt integrity does not establish
factual correctness or independent settlement. Gateway batching need not provide a
separate onchain transaction for each individual payment. Unknown/pending remains so.

Never create repeated transactions to manufacture traction. Actual operating invoices,
customer data and internal profit stay private. Testnet tokens are not real revenue.

## First product increment

Add a read-only evidence browser before the paid package on `/research`. Reuse existing
public source/preview APIs; show listing/search limits, public metadata and honest
empty/error states. It must not purchase, register, fund, score answerability or change
source/payment authority. This helps a participant decide whether a trial is relevant;
it does not solve the missing-corpus problem or demonstrate either product direction.

Implementation checks and external validation are separate. Ship only after focused
browser behavior, TypeScript, lint, production build, required CI and review pass.
Follow the repository PR/release workflow; coordinate deployment with concurrent
release owners. Mainnet launch and real-fund spend remain separately authorized.

## Direction selection

Prefer research when repeated useful outcomes and credible purchasing behavior are
observed. Prefer infrastructure when independent providers maintain integrations and
obtain genuine buyer use. If both work, retain a research reference application over
shared focused primitives. If neither works, keep the direction open and investigate
the unmet task instead of expanding surfaces.
